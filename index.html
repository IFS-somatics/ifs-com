Internal Family Systems (IFS)

"We all have different parts within us,

and there are no bad parts"

Some parts may struggle to cope with everyday life, others are full of anxiety and addictive coping strategies, and some may be holding you back from achieving your deepest life dreams.


And all they really want is to finally be fully seen, heard, and embraced.


I offer a space where all of your parts are welcome and where we can take the time to truly be with them.

About IFS


IFS starts from the premise that each of us has a healthy core of consciousness with qualities like compassion, calmness, and curiosity. This healthy Self is like the sun behind the clouds — it’s always there.


IFS recognises that we're all made up of multiple parts — distinct aspects of our inner world, each with its own perspective, feelings, and role. Due to early experiences, we've often had to suppress certain aspects of ourselves and disconnect from our authentic Self to fit in. Consequently, we lose touch with who we truly are.


Rather than viewing our parts as pathological when they exhibit extreme behaviours — such as intense anger, self-criticism, or self-destructive patterns — IFS suggests that these parts serve a protective function. They shield us from feeling the painful experiences we've been through.

These painful experiences are held by what IFS calls Exiles — parts that carry the pain of what happened to us, often from childhood. Protectors are the parts that developed to keep that pain from surfacing, whether by pushing it away, numbing it, or distracting us from it.


IFS helps us get to know and connect with the different parts of our inner system, and to heal and unburden them from trauma or false beliefs — restoring connection, vitality, and joy in your life.

Why IFS and somatics?


Somatics is the practice of paying close attention to the body - its sensations, movements, posture, breath and impulses - as a direct source of information, rather than something the mind simply reports on afterwards. Where talk-based work moves through story and language, somatic work moves through what the body is already doing, right now, in the room.


This matters a great deal for parts work. Many of our parts, especially the youngest and most tender ones, formed before we had words for what was happening to us. These pre-verbal parts don't carry their story as a narrative we can simply ask about — they carry it as sensation, muscular bracing, a held breath, a collapse in the chest, an urge to freeze or flee. Somatics gives us a way to meet these parts on their own terms, through the body's own language, instead of asking them to translate themselves into words they were never given.


When we slow down and follow a movement, a posture or a gesture as it wants to unfold — rather than overriding or ignoring it — it often reveals the intention or need underneath it. A hand that wants to push away may be showing us a boundary that was never allowed. A slight curling inward may be protecting something vulnerable. The body knows what it was trying to do all along; our work together is to give that impulse enough safety and space to complete itself.

Many of us carry actions that were interrupted long ago — a protest that couldn't be voiced, a run that couldn't be taken, a reach that was never met. These unfinished impulses often stay held in the body as chronic tension, bracing patterns or energetic blocks, quietly shaping how we move through the world. By noticing these patterns somatically and allowing the original impulse to complete — often in small, titrated movements — the nervous system can finally register that the old danger has passed, and settle into a new level of regulation.



Six doorways into the body


Much of how I bring the body into IFS work draws on Susan McConnell's Somatic IFS as well as from practices like Authentic Movement, Vipassana, breathwork and BMC.


  • The felt sense — cultivating a felt, embodied awareness. Experiencing the body from within.
  • Breath — using the breath to track activation and settling, and to gently support a part's nervous system state.
  • Attunement — sensing another person's or a part's state directly in your own body.
  • Impulse/Movement — letting posture, gesture and impulse speak, and following where they want to go.
  • Touch — when appropriate and consented to, using touch to bring warmth, containment or grounding to a part.
  • Voice/Sound — allowing tone, volume and sound to express what a part hasn't yet found words for.


Together, these tools let us work with parts that talking alone can't always reach — especially the youngest ones — while staying rooted in the compassionate, curious presence of Self.

    What a first session is like


    We'll begin by simply arriving — slowing down, and noticing what's here. You don't need to have anything figured out, and you're welcome to bring as much or as little as you like.


    From there, we follow what wants your attention, gently, and at a pace that feels right for all of your parts.

    How often, and how long


    Sessions are 60 or 90 minutes. Sixty minutes is enough to settle in and work with something meaningful. Ninety gives more room for deeper work — unburdening in particular tends to want the extra time. If you'd like more time than that, longer sessions can be arranged.


    There's no required commitment and no fixed number of sessions. Some people come with a particular thing to work through and find a handful of sessions is enough; others stay longer because the work keeps unfolding, or because they want a committed space they can return to over time. 


    For many people, once a week or once every other week works well. Coming back session after session builds a skillset over time — and you will find it easier to embrace your parts as they show up in everyday life.


    We can talk about what suits you as we go.

    Psychedelic preparation and integration

    Some of the most meaningful experiences of our lives happen outside ordinary states of consciousness — and they can leave us with more than we know how to carry. A powerful opening can be followed by confusion, or a quiet sense that something shifted but hasn't yet landed. Sometimes there's simply a lot of energy still stuck in the system, waiting for a chance to unfold and be integrated.


    Preparation is where we get to know the terrain in advance. What are you hoping for? What are you afraid of? Which parts of you are eager, and which are holding back? We work somatically with the anticipation itself, so that anxiety has somewhere to go and your system has resources to draw on. Preparation isn't about controlling what happens — it's about arriving with more of yourself present.

    Integration is the slower work that follows. Rather than re-telling the story of what happened, we listen for what the experience is still doing in your body and your inner system — the parts it touched, the places still humming. Integration is often where the real change takes root, as insight gradually becomes something you live from rather than something you remember.


    This work sits alongside, rather than replaces, medical or psychiatric care. I don't facilitate or supply any substance, and I don't make diagnoses. What I offer is a grounded, trauma-informed space to make sense of what you've been through or what you're preparing for.

    Authentic Breath


    One of our main ways to avoid unwanted feelings is to control our breath.

    Often in a very early stage in life we learn that certain feelings like fear, sadness or anger are not wanted. From the fear of being abandoned we try to avoid these feelings by contracting the diaphragm, chest and surrounding muscles and stopping the emotion from reaching our vital body. As adults many of us still subconsciously suppress certain feelings by not allowing our breath to run free. We can also find our fascia in the chest and solar plexus to be stagnated and blocked after a lifetime of repeating the same patterns, making it difficult to even physically take a full breath.


    In this workshop you are invited to form a new relationship and awareness with your breath, learning how to allow the natural expansion and contraction of the lungs to lead the way for how your breath and body actually wants to regulate its emotional state in the present moment.


    Authentic breath is informed by practices like Authentic Movement, Vipassana meditation and various breath work practices. It’s a somatic practice based on embodiment, sensory awareness and a trauma aware release of unexpressed feelings. We will work with witnessing and being witnessed as our stored emotions and tensions naturally unwind.

    IFS Practitioner & Somatic educator

    I help clients reconnect with their bodies, support nervous system regulation, and cultivate Self-leadership and self-compassion. Through Internal Family Systems (IFS) and trauma-informed somatic practices, I guide people in exploring emotions, inner conflicts, and accessing their natural resilience.


    My journey began in 2011 with deep embodiment practices like Authentic Movement, Contact Improvisation, Butoh, Holotropic Breathwork, and Vipassana. As these practices brought up emotions and earlier life fragments, they inspired me to dive deeper and explore trauma-informed somatic approaches such as Somatic Experiencing, Polyvagal Theory, and NARM. Eventually, I discovered IFS, which became the missing piece in integrating bottom-up and top-down approaches for deep personal transformation.


    This integrative approach allows me to meet clients in a grounded, attuned way, honouring both the intelligence of the body and the wisdom of our inner parts.
